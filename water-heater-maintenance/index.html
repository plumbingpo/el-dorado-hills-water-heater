The inside of a neglected water heater tells the whole story. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in El Dorado Hills, CA, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. At the end of any visit, you get a plain-language report of what we found. Call (279) 242-2736 to schedule water heater maintenance in El Dorado Hills before a small issue turns into a leak.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in El Dorado Hills

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Maintenance

The homeowner's side of maintenance is short: keep the area clear, watch for drips, and pay attention to new sounds. The rest carries more risk than it looks. Flushing involves scalding water and an old plastic drain valve that may crack or refuse to seal. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
