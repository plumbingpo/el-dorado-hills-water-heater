Keeping Your Folsom Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Well before a tank floods a Folsom garage, it almost always sends signals. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
