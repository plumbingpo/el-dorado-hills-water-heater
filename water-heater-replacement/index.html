Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
